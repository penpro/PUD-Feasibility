# Findings

**As of 2026-11-04. Preliminary: Module 1 (Load) only.** Full numbers, inputs and provenance are in
[`modules/01_load/results.md`](../modules/01_load/results.md), generated by `python -m model.load`.
Nothing here says whether KPUD *should* provide electric service. These findings size the load such a
utility would have to serve.

## F-01: PSE's Kitsap load is about 2.0 TWh a year, not 3.2 TWh (ESTIMATE)

| 2025 | Low | Central | High | Stress range |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Annual retail energy (TWh) | 1.74 | **2.04** | 2.60 | 1.52–2.66 |
| Average load at meter (MW) | 199 | **233** | 297 | 173–303 |
| Average load incl. losses (MW) | 206 | **242** | 315 | |
| Annual peak (MW) | 342 | **471** | 738 | |

Two independent methods land within about 10% of each other: a back-calculation from the county's 2022 GHG
inventory (2.15 TWh, 2025-scaled) and a bottom-up customer × benchmark build (1.94 TWh). The main
uncertainty is which emission factor the inventory used. A public records request for the underlying
kWh would resolve it (OQ-01).

## F-02: The 3.24 TWh figure is a statewide average scaled by population (VERIFIED arithmetic, UNVERIFIED source)

FindEnergy's Kitsap total (3,236,601 MWh) ÷ its population (277,673) = 11.66 MWh/person. That is
FindEnergy's figure for **all of Washington**, and the same 11.66 appears on its other county pages. The
statewide average includes data centers, pulp mills and irrigation loads Kitsap does not have. Even a
correct county total would also include federal Navy load served directly by BPA (S013) and
Peninsula Light and Mason PUD 3 customers, none of which KPUD would acquire from PSE. **Do not use
3.24 TWh, or the ~370 MW average derived from it.** It is 58% above this study's central estimate and
22% above the stress-high bound.

## F-03: "Kitsap needs around 370 megawatts" fits a peak, not an average (UNVERIFIED claim, tested)

KUOW (S016) gave 370 MW without saying average or peak and without a source. As an average it is above
every plausible case (max ~303 MW). As a peak it falls inside this study's peak range (342–738 MW). This
matters for power supply: BPA Tier 1 allocations are sized in average megawatts, while substations and
transmission are sized to peak.

## F-04: Peak demand is the weakest number and the most important missing data (ESTIMATE)

The peak range (342–738 MW) comes from neighboring utilities' load factors, not Kitsap measurements, and
it is not weather-normalized. PSE's Kitsap-area peak (in its non-wires-alternatives RFP, area load
forecasts or UTC filings) is the top data priority after kWh (OQ-05).

## F-05: Load grows to roughly 2.0–4.3 TWh by 2045 (ASSUMPTION-driven)

Central 2.89 TWh in 2045 (+41%): population growth (OFM), +10% per-customer electrification net of
efficiency, and ~110,000 EVs (up from 10,383 registered plug-in vehicles in Oct 2026, S012). The growth
inputs are labeled ASSUMPTION until gas-therm and vehicle-registration data replace them.
